Know what you’re comparing

Open-cell or closed-cell?

Foam type, installed thickness and the building assembly all matter. Confirm the specific product and its data sheet with Eric before choosing a specification.

Open-cell foam

Softer, lower-density foam that is more permeable to moisture vapor.

Typically R-3.6–4.5 per inch

A lower R-value per inch generally means more thickness is needed to reach the same insulation target.

Closed-cell foam

Denser, rigid foam with higher thermal resistance per inch and greater resistance to moisture movement.

Typically R-5.8–6.9 per inch

Useful where available depth is limited. The required thickness and vapor-control strategy depend on the product and assembly.

General foam information, not a statement of Silverback’s stocked products. R-values vary by product. Source: Spray Polyurethane Foam Alliance.

What affects the cost?

Floor area alone doesn’t tell the whole story. A quote needs to account for the surfaces being sprayed, their height and the specified foam thickness.

Straight answers

Before you insulate.

What should I send for an estimate?

Your town, building type, length, width and wall height. Add interior and exterior photos, which walls or roof areas you want covered, and whether the space will be heated or cooled.

Can spray foam fix a leaking roof?

Do not treat interior insulation as a roof-leak repair. Identify and repair water entry before covering surfaces, and include the building’s moisture conditions in the insulation plan.

How much R-value do I need?

That depends on your building’s use, assembly and applicable requirements. Ask for the product name, specified thickness and rated performance in your estimate. Foam type alone is not a complete specification.

Can the foam remain exposed?

The building use and assembly determine the required fire protection. Ask whether a thermal barrier, ignition barrier or approved coating is included in the quote. Read SPFA’s guidance.

When can I use the building again?

Return time depends on the product and job conditions, including ventilation. Follow the installer’s specific re-entry and re-occupancy instructions rather than assuming a fixed time.

Is Silverback insured?

Silverback identifies its business as fully insured. Request current proof of insurance and any project-specific credentials before work begins.
